Group A — Multiple Choice Questions [11 × 1 = 11 marks]

1. Which physical quantity has the dimensional formula \([M L^{-1} T^{-2}]\)?
a. Work    b. Power    c. Pressure    d. Force
Answer: c. Pressure
Reason: Pressure \(= \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}\)
\([P] = \frac{[MLT^{-2}]}{[L^2]} = [ML^{-1}T^{-2}]\)
2. If the vector \(\vec A = 3\hat i + 4\hat j\), its magnitude is:
a. 5    b. 7    c. 25    d. None of these
Answer: a. 5
\(|\vec A| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5\)
3. The SI unit of impulse is same as that of:
a. Force    b. Momentum    c. Energy    d. Power
Answer: b. Momentum
Reason: Impulse, \(I = F \times t\). Unit: \(N s = kg\,m\,s^{-1}\), which is also the SI unit of momentum.
4. A 2 kg object moving at 3 m/s strikes a wall and stops in 0.1 s. The average force exerted by the wall is:
a. 6 N    b. 30 N    c. 60 N    d. 120 N
Answer: c. 60 N
Given, \(m = 2\,kg,\quad u = 3\,m/s,\quad v = 0,\quad t = 0.1\,s\)
Average force, \(F = \frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{m(v-u)}{t} = \frac{2(0-3)}{0.1} = -60\,N\)
Magnitude of force: \(F = 60\,N\)
5. In uniform circular motion, which of the following quantities remains constant?
a. 0°    b. 45°    c. 90°    d. 180°
Answer: Speed remains constant.
Explanation: The options provided in the question paper are incorrect/printing mistakes. In uniform circular motion, the magnitude of velocity (speed) remains constant but the direction of velocity changes continuously.
6. Work done is maximum when the angle between force and displacement is:
a. 0°    b. 45°    c. 90°    d. 180°
Answer: a. 0°
Work done, \(W = Fs\cos\theta\). Work is maximum when \(\cos\theta\) is maximum. \(\cos 0^\circ = 1\). Therefore, work done is maximum when force and displacement are in the same direction.
7. A particle of mass \(m\) has momentum \(p\). What is its kinetic energy?
a. 2mp    b. \(\frac{p^2}{2m}\)    c. 2mp²    d. \(\frac{2m}{p^2}\)
Answer: b. \(\frac{p^2}{2m}\)
We know, \(p = mv \implies v = \frac{p}{m}\)
Kinetic energy, \(K.E. = \frac12 mv^2 = \frac12 m\left(\frac{p}{m}\right)^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}\)
8. At what height above the Earth's surface does the acceleration due to gravity become one-fourth of its value on the surface?
a. R/4    b. R/2    c. R    d. 2R
Answer: c. R
At height \(h\), \(g_h = g\left(\frac{R}{R+h}\right)^2\). Given \(g_h = \frac g4\):
\(\frac g4 = g\left(\frac{R}{R+h}\right)^2 \implies \frac12 = \frac{R}{R+h} \implies R+h = 2R \implies h = R\)
9. What is the value of bulk modulus for an incompressible liquid?
a. Zero    b. Less than 1    c. Unity    d. Infinity
Answer: d. Infinity
For an incompressible liquid, volume change is zero. \(K = -\frac{\text{Pressure stress}}{\text{Volume strain}}\). If volume strain is zero, bulk modulus becomes infinite.
10. If the emissivity \(e\) of a body is less than 1, the body is considered:
a. A perfect black body    b. Not a perfectly black body    c. An impossible body    d. A perfect reflectctor
Answer: b. Not a perfectly black body
A perfect black body has emissivity \(e = 1\). If \(e < 1\), the body is not a perfect black body.
11. If a ray of light is incident normally on a plane surface, the angle of refraction is:
a. 90°    b. 45°    c. 180°    d. 0°
Answer: d. 0°
If the ray is incident normally, angle of incidence is zero. Therefore, angle of refraction is also zero.

Group B — Short Answer Questions [8 × 5 = 40 marks]

1. (a) State the principle of homogeneity. Find the dimension of K in the equation \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{K}}\). [2+3]
Principle of homogeneity: The principle of homogeneity states that in any correct physical equation, the dimensions of all terms on both sides must be the same.

Dimension of K:
Given, \(T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{K}}\). Here, \(2\pi\) is dimensionless.
Squaring both sides: \(T^2 = \frac{m}{K} \implies K = \frac{m}{T^2}\)
Dimension of mass \([m] = [M]\), Dimension of time \([T] = [T]\)
Therefore, \([K] = \frac{[M]}{[T^2]} = [MT^{-2}]\)
2. (a) State the triangle law of vector addition. Two vectors \(\vec A\) and \(\vec B\) are such that \(\vec A - \vec B = \vec C\) and \(A = B = C\). Find the angle between them. [2+3]
Triangle law of vector addition: If two vectors are represented by two sides of a triangle taken in the same order, then their resultant is represented by the third side of the triangle taken in the opposite order.

Angle calculation:
Given, \(\vec A - \vec B = \vec C\) and \(A = B = C\). Let the angle between \(\vec A\) and \(\vec B\) be \(\theta\).
Taking magnitude and squaring: \(C^2 = A^2 + B^2 - 2AB\cos\theta\)
Since \(A = B = C = a\):
\(a^2 = a^2 + a^2 - 2a^2\cos\theta \implies a^2 = 2a^2 - 2a^2\cos\theta\)
Dividing by \(a^2\): \(1 = 2 - 2\cos\theta \implies 2\cos\theta = 1 \implies \cos\theta = \frac12\)
Therefore, \(\theta = 60^\circ\)
3. (a) What is centripetal acceleration? Derive an expression for centripetal acceleration. [1+4]
Centripetal acceleration: The acceleration directed towards the centre of a circular path in uniform circular motion is called centripetal acceleration.

Derivation:
Let a body move in a circle of radius \(r\) with uniform speed \(v\). Suppose the body moves from point A to B in small time \(\Delta t\). The velocity changes from \(\vec v_1\) to \(\vec v_2\) (magnitude same, direction changes).
Change in velocity \(\Delta v = v\Delta\theta\) (for small angular displacement \(\Delta \theta\)).
Also, arc length \(\Delta s = r\Delta\theta\). But \(\Delta s = v\Delta t\), so \(\Delta\theta = \frac{v\Delta t}{r}\).
Substituting: \(\Delta v = v\left(\frac{v\Delta t}{r}\right) = \frac{v^2\Delta t}{r}\)
Acceleration, \(a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v^2}{r}\)
Thus, centripetal acceleration \(a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}\). Since \(v = r\omega\), it can also be written as \(a_c = r\omega^2\).
4. (a) State and prove the Work-Energy theorem. [2+3]
Statement: The work-energy theorem states that the work done by a net force on a body is equal to the change in its kinetic energy. (\(W = \Delta K.E.\))

Proof:
Let a body of mass \(m\) move with initial velocity \(u\). A constant force \(F\) acts on it through displacement \(s\), and its final velocity becomes \(v\).
Work done, \(W = Fs\). According to Newton’s second law, \(F = ma\). So, \(W = mas\).
From the equation of motion: \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as \implies as = \frac{v^2-u^2}{2}\)
Therefore, \(W = m\left(\frac{v^2-u^2}{2}\right) = \frac12 mv^2 - \frac12 mu^2 = K.E._f - K.E._i = \Delta K.E.\)
Hence proved.
5. (a) Define gravitational potential energy and explain why it is negative. Derive an expression for it. [1+4]
Definition: The gravitational potential energy of a body at a point in a gravitational field is the work done in bringing the body from infinity to that point without acceleration.

Why negative: Gravitational force is attractive. The zero level of gravitational potential energy is taken at infinity. When a body is brought from infinity to a point near earth, its potential energy decreases. Therefore, it is negative.

Derivation:
Let a mass \(m\) be brought from infinity to a distance \(r\) from mass \(M\).
Gravitational force, \(F = \frac{GMm}{r^2}\)
Work done (Potential Energy), \(U = -\int_{\infty}^{r} \frac{GMm}{r^2}\,dr = -GMm\left[-\frac1r\right]_{\infty}^{r} = -\frac{GMm}{r}\)
6. (a) Define Poisson's ratio. Derive the expression for it and find the expression for escape velocity of earth. [2+3]
Poisson’s ratio (\(\sigma\)): It is defined as the ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain within elastic limit.
\(\sigma = -\frac{\text{lateral strain}}{\text{longitudinal strain}} = -\frac{\Delta D/D}{\Delta L/L}\)

Escape Velocity Derivation:
Escape velocity is the minimum velocity with which a body must be projected from the surface of earth so that it just escapes the gravitational field.
At earth’s surface, Gravitational P.E. = \(-\frac{GMm}{R}\) and K.E. = \(\frac12 mv_e^2\)
For escape, Total energy = 0 \(\implies \frac12 mv_e^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} = 0 \implies v_e^2 = \frac{2GM}{R}\)
\(v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}\). Since \(g = \frac{GM}{R^2}\), we get \(v_e = \sqrt{2gR}\).
7. (a) Define moment of couple (torque). Find the expression for work done due to couple. (b) A cricketer moves his hand while catching the ball — why? Explain. [3+2]
Moment of couple: A couple consists of two equal and opposite parallel forces acting at different points. The moment of couple (torque) is the product of one force and the perpendicular distance between them: \(\tau = Fd\).
Work done by couple: If a couple produces angular displacement \(\theta\), work done is \(W = \tau \theta\).

(b) Cricketer moving hands: A cricketer moves his hands backward while catching a fast ball to increase the time of impact (\(\Delta t\)). Impulse \(F \Delta t = \Delta p\). For a fixed change in momentum, if \(\Delta t\) increases, the average force \(F\) decreases, hurting the hands less.
8. (a) State Newton's law of cooling. (b) A steel wire 2 mm in diameter is stretched between two clamps. When its temperature falls to 30°C, calculate the tension. [\(\alpha_s = 11\times10^{-6} °C^{-1}, Y_s = 21\times10^{10} dyne/cm^2\)] [2+3]
Newton’s law of cooling: The rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the excess temperature of the body over the surroundings, provided the temperature difference is small. (\(\frac{dQ}{dt} \propto (T - T_s)\))

(b) Numerical:
Given: \(d = 2\,mm = 0.2\,cm \implies r = 0.1\,cm\). Area \(A = \pi r^2 = 0.0314\,cm^2\).
Assuming the temperature falls by \(\Delta T = 30^\circ C\).
Thermal stress = \(Y\alpha \Delta T\)
Tension, \(F = \text{Stress} \times A = Y\alpha \Delta T \times A\)
\(F = 21 \times 10^{10} \times 11 \times 10^{-6} \times 30 \times 0.0314 = 2.18 \times 10^6\,dyne\)
Since \(1\,N = 10^5\,dyne\), \(F = \frac{2.18 \times 10^6}{10^5} = 21.8\,N \approx 22\,N\)
9. (a) Why do pendulum clocks made of brass keep correct time at 15°C but lose time at higher temperatures? How many seconds per day will it lose at 20°C? [For brass \(\alpha = 0.000018 °C^{-1}\)] [2+3]
Why it loses time: The time period of a simple pendulum is \(T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}\). At higher temperatures, the brass rod expands, so length \(l\) increases. Since \(T \propto \sqrt{l}\), the time period increases, making the clock slow.

Numerical:
\(\Delta T = 20 - 15 = 5^\circ C\)
Loss of time per day = \(\frac12 \alpha \Delta T \times 86400\)
\(= \frac12 \times 0.000018 \times 5 \times 86400 = 3.888\,s \approx 3.9\,s/day\)
10. (a) What is weightlessness? Derive an expression for escape velocity. (b) What is Angular displacement? Derive an expression for centripetal acceleration. [2+3]
Weightlessness: Weightlessness is the condition in which the apparent weight of a body becomes zero (normal reaction is zero). E.g., an astronaut in a freely falling satellite.
Escape velocity: As derived in Q6, \(v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2gR}\).

Angular displacement: Angular displacement is the angle swept by the radius vector of a particle moving in a circular path. \(\theta = \frac{s}{r}\).
Centripetal acceleration: As derived in Q3, \(a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = r\omega^2\).

Group C — Long Answer Questions [3 × 8 = 24 marks]

1. Projectile Motion
(a) Show that the path of a projectile fired from the ground by making angle \(\theta\) with horizontal is parabolic. [3]
(b) Find the angle of projection at which horizontal range and maximum height is equal. [3]
(c) Find the distance, displacement and acceleration at 20 seconds using the v-t graph given. [2]
(a) Parabolic Path Derivation:
Let a projectile be projected with initial velocity \(u\) at an angle \(\theta\).
Horizontal component: \(u_x = u\cos\theta\). Vertical component: \(u_y = u\sin\theta\).
Horizontal displacement at time \(t\): \(x = u\cos\theta \, t \implies t = \frac{x}{u\cos\theta}\)
Vertical displacement at time \(t\): \(y = u\sin\theta \, t - \frac12 gt^2\)
Substituting \(t\):
\(y = u\sin\theta \left(\frac{x}{u\cos\theta}\right) -\frac12 g\left(\frac{x}{u\cos\theta}\right)^2\)
\(y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}\)
This is of the form \(y = ax - bx^2\), which is the equation of a parabola.

(b) Angle when Range = Max Height:
Horizontal Range \(R = \frac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}\). Maximum Height \(H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}\).
Given \(R = H\):
\(\frac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g} = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g} \implies \sin 2\theta = \frac{\sin^2\theta}{2}\)
\(2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \frac{\sin^2\theta}{2} \implies 4\sin\theta\cos\theta = \sin^2\theta\)
\(4\cos\theta = \sin\theta \implies \tan\theta = 4\)
\(\theta = \tan^{-1}(4) \approx 76^\circ\)

(c) From v-t graph:
Note: The v-t graph is missing in the question paper.
Conceptually, from any velocity-time graph:
1. Displacement = Algebraic area under the v-t graph.
2. Distance = Total area under the speed-time graph (absolute area).
3. Acceleration = Slope of the v-t graph (\(a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}\)).

OR: Ball thrown upward
Given: \(u = 20\,m/s\), \(v = 0\) at max height, \(g = 10\,m/s^2\)
Using \(v^2 = u^2 - 2gH\):
\(0 = 20^2 - 2 \times 10 \times H \implies 20H = 400 \implies H = 20\,m\)
2. Satellite and Orbital Velocity
(a) A satellite revolves close to the surface of a planet. How is its orbital velocity related to the velocity of escape from that planet? Derive an expression for orbital velocity. [4]
(b) A satellite revolves close to the surface of the planet. Calculate the magnitude and direction of velocity after 5 seconds. [2]
(c) An earth satellite moves in a circular orbit at distance 3400 km from earth. Find the period of revolution and orbital velocity. [R = 6400 km, g = 9.8 m/s²] [2]
(a) Derivation & Relation:
Let a satellite of mass \(m\) revolve around a planet of mass \(M\) in a circular orbit of radius \(r\). Gravitational force provides centripetal force:
\(\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv_o^2}{r} \implies v_o^2 = \frac{GM}{r} \implies v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}\)
For a satellite close to the surface, \(r = R\), so \(v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}}\).
Escape velocity \(v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2} \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2}\,v_o\).
Therefore, \(v_e = \sqrt{2}\,v_o\) or \(v_o = \frac{v_e}{\sqrt{2}}\).

(b) Velocity after 5 seconds:
Note: Numerical data is missing in the question.
Conceptually, for uniform circular motion: The magnitude of velocity remains constant. The direction of velocity is always tangential to the circular path at that specific point. So after 5 seconds, it will be tangential to its new position.

(c) Numerical:
Assuming \(h = 3400\,km\), \(R = 6400\,km\). Orbital radius \(r = R + h = 9800\,km = 9.8 \times 10^6\,m\).
Orbital velocity \(v_o = \sqrt{\frac{gR^2}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{9.8 \times (6.4 \times 10^6)^2}{9.8 \times 10^6}} = \sqrt{(6.4)^2 \times 10^6} = 6400\,m/s\)
Time period \(T = \frac{2\pi r}{v_o} = \frac{2\pi \times 9.8 \times 10^6}{6400} \approx 9620\,s \approx 160\,minutes\).
3. Concave Mirror and Optics
(a) Derive the mirror formula of a concave mirror. [4]
(b) The radius of curvature of a concave mirror is 24 cm and the image is magnified by 1.5 times. What is the object distance? [2]
(c) Define linear magnification and express it in terms of image distance and object distance. [2]
(a) Mirror Formula Derivation:
The mirror formula is \(\frac1f = \frac1v + \frac1u\).
Consider an object placed beyond \(C\) in front of a concave mirror. A ray parallel to the principal axis passes through \(F\) after reflection. Another ray passing through \(C\) retraces its path. These rays intersect at point \(A'\) (image).
Using similar triangles in the ray diagram and applying sign conventions (distances against incident ray are negative):
\(\frac{A'B}{AB} = \frac{A'F}{PF}\) and \(\frac{A'B}{AB} = \frac{A'C}{PC}\)
Substituting signed values (\(AB = +h_1, A'B = -h_2, PF = -f, PC = -R, A'F = v-f, A'C = v-R\)) and solving using \(R = 2f\) leads to: \(\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}\)

(b) Object Distance:
\(R = 24\,cm \implies f = -12\,cm\). Magnification \(m = -1.5\) (assuming real image).
\(m = -\frac vu \implies -1.5 = -\frac vu \implies v = 1.5u\)
Using mirror formula: \(\frac1{-12} = \frac1{1.5u} + \frac1u \implies -\frac1{12} = \frac{2}{3u} + \frac1u = \frac{5}{3u}\)
\(3u = -60 \implies u = -20\,cm\). Object distance is 20 cm from the mirror.

(c) Linear Magnification:
Linear magnification is defined as the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object.
\(m = \frac{h_i}{h_o}\). In terms of distances, \(m = -\frac vu\).

OR: Prism
(a) Minimum Deviation: Minimum deviation is the least angle through which a ray of light is deviated by a prism. Conditions: 1) Angle of incidence = Angle of emergence (\(i = e\)). 2) The ray passes symmetrically through the prism (\(r_1 = r_2 = A/2\)).

(b) Derivation:
For a prism, \(A = r_1 + r_2\). At minimum deviation, \(r_1 = r_2 = r \implies A = 2r \implies r = A/2\).
Also, \(D_m = i + e - A\). Since \(i = e\), \(D_m = 2i - A \implies i = \frac{A+D_m}{2}\).
Using Snell’s law at the first surface: \(\mu = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r} \implies \mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A+D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac A2\right)}\)

(c) Numerical:
Relative refractive index \(\mu = \frac{\mu_g}{\mu_l} = \frac{1.66}{1.33} \approx 1.248\).
Angle of refraction \(r = \frac A2 = \frac{72^\circ}{2} = 36^\circ\).
Using the formula: \(1.248 = \frac{\sin(\frac{72^\circ+D_m}{2})}{\sin36^\circ} \implies \sin(\frac{72^\circ+D_m}{2}) = 1.248 \times 0.588 = 0.734\)
\(\frac{72^\circ+D_m}{2} = \sin^{-1}(0.734) \approx 47.2^\circ \implies 72^\circ + D_m = 94.4^\circ \implies D_m \approx 22.4^\circ\)
4. Thermal Expansion and Heat
(a) Define an element of an electric fire with an output of 1.5 kW. It is a cylinder of 0.3 m long and 0.04 m in radius. Calculate its temperature if it behaves as a black body. [3]
(b) Define temperature gradient. Derive the relation between thermal conductivity and temperature gradient. [2]
(c) A glass vessel of volume 50 cm³ is filled with mercury and is heated from 20°C to 60°C. What volume of mercury will overflow? [\(\alpha_g = 1.8\times10^{-6} K^{-1}\) and \(\gamma_m = ?\)] [3]
(a) Black Body Temperature:
Given: \(P = 1500\,W\), \(l = 0.3\,m\), \(r = 0.04\,m\), \(\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\,Wm^{-2}K^{-4}\).
Surface area of cylinder \(A = 2\pi r(l+r) = 2\pi(0.04)(0.34) \approx 0.0854\,m^2\).
For a black body, \(P = \sigma A T^4 \implies T^4 = \frac{P}{\sigma A} = \frac{1500}{5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times 0.0854} \approx 3.1 \times 10^{11}\)
\(T \approx 746\,K\) (or \(473^\circ C\))

(b) Temperature Gradient:
Temperature gradient is the rate of change of temperature with distance in the direction of heat flow (\(\frac{dT}{dx}\)).
From Fourier's law of heat conduction: \(\frac{Q}{t} = -KA\frac{dT}{dx}\)
Thus, thermal conductivity \(K = \frac{H}{A|\frac{dT}{dx}|}\), where \(H\) is the rate of heat flow. The negative sign indicates heat flows from high to low temperature.

(c) Overflow Volume:
Given: \(V = 50\,cm^3\), \(\Delta T = 40^\circ C\). \(\gamma_g = 3\alpha_g = 3 \times 1.8 \times 10^{-6} = 5.4 \times 10^{-6}K^{-1}\).
Standard value for mercury \(\gamma_m = 1.8 \times 10^{-4}K^{-1}\).
Overflow volume \(\Delta V = V(\gamma_m - \gamma_g)\Delta T\)
\(\Delta V = 50(1.8 \times 10^{-4} - 5.4 \times 10^{-6}) \times 40 = 50(1.746 \times 10^{-4}) \times 40 = 0.3492\,cm^3 \approx 0.35\,cm^3\)
5. Gravitational PE, Conservation of Energy & Circular Motion
(a) Derive the mathematical expression for gravitational potential energy. [3]
(b) Derive the orbital velocity, time period and total energy of satellite. [3]
(c) If the force on all bodies is proportional to their masses, why does a heavy body fall faster than a light body? [2]
(a) Gravitational PE Derivation:
Let a body of mass \(m\) be at a distance \(r\) from the centre of earth of mass \(M\).
Gravitational force \(F = \frac{GMm}{r^2}\).
Gravitational potential energy at distance \(r\) is the work done in bringing the body from infinity to that point:
\(U = -\int_{\infty}^{r} F\,dr = -\int_{\infty}^{r} \frac{GMm}{r^2}\,dr = -GMm\left[-\frac1r\right]_{\infty}^{r} = -\frac{GMm}{r}\)

(b) Satellite Derivations:
**Orbital velocity:** \(\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \implies v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}\)
**Time period:** \(T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi r}{\sqrt{GM/r}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}\)
**Total energy:** K.E. = \(\frac12 mv^2 = \frac{GMm}{2r}\). P.E. = \(-\frac{GMm}{r}\).
Total Energy \(E = K.E. + P.E. = \frac{GMm}{2r} - \frac{GMm}{r} = -\frac{GMm}{2r}\)

(c) Heavy vs Light Body Falling:
In vacuum, a heavy body and a light body fall with the same acceleration because \(F = mg \implies a = \frac{F}{m} = g\) (acceleration is independent of mass).
However, in air, a light body is more affected by air resistance (buoyancy and drag) compared to its weight. A heavy body overcomes air resistance more easily due to its greater weight, causing it to fall faster in real-world conditions.
